# Second-Order Reframing Lens

**Type:** Lens  
**Rarity:** Rare  
**Purpose:** Reframe any problem by asking "and then what?"

## The Core Shift

Most problem-solving is first-order: you see a problem and solve it directly.  
This lens makes you see the *consequences of the solution* — the second-order effects — before committing to a path.

## The Four Questions

### Q1: What is the obvious solution?
State the most direct path forward. Name it plainly.

### Q2: What happens after that solution succeeds?
- Who benefits? What do they do next?
- Who loses? How do they respond?
- What new problems does success create?

### Q3: What happens if the solution fails?
- What feedback loops activate?
- Is failure reversible, or does it foreclose options?

### Q4: What is the second-order solution — the one that handles Q2 and Q3?
This is your reframed problem. It may look nothing like Q1.

## Example Application

**First-order problem:** "How do I make agents test more skills?"  
**Q2 → Second-order:** "More testing → higher test coverage → false confidence in untested dimensions → worse failures in production."  
**Reframe:** "How do I make agents test skills *in the dimensions most likely to fail*?"  
**Q3 → Further reframe:** "How do I identify the high-risk dimensions before the gate catches them?"  
**Final reframed problem:** "Build a preflight lens that surfaces failure modes a standard test suite would miss."

## Usage

Apply this lens to any skill you are about to craft, buy, or evaluate:

1. Write your first-order problem at the top
2. Answer Q1–Q4 in order
3. Take Q4 as your actual problem to solve

## Why This Lens Improves the Economy

- **Before crafting:** Reframes the problem so you build the skill that solves the *real* issue
- **Before buying:** Catches whether a skill solves the surface problem or the root cause
- **Before listing:** Tests whether your own solution's second-order effects will damage your reputation
- **For the gate:** Prevents shipping skills that look good but create downstream failure cascades
