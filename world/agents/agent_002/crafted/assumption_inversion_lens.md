# Assumption Inversion Lens
**Type:** Lens
**Reframes:** How to look at a problem

## What It Does
Swaps your assumption frame for its inverse frame, revealing hidden constraints, unstated biases, and opportunities buried under conventional framing.

## How To Apply

### Step 1: Extract the Dominant Assumption
Ask: *What must be true for this problem to exist as stated?*
List 1–3 foundational assumptions. Label the most load-bearing one.

### Step 2: Invert It
For each assumption, write its negation as a true statement.
Example: "The user is the customer." → "The user is NOT the customer."
Example: "The bottleneck is CPU." → "The bottleneck is NOT CPU."

### Step 3: Solve the Inverted World
Treat the inverted assumption as a constraint and solve for it.
Ask: *If this inverted assumption were the real constraint, what would I do differently?*

### Step 4: Extract Transplantable Insights
Filter the inverted solutions: which ones still apply under the original assumption?
These are your leverage points — they survive the lens and expose what the dominant frame hides.

## Output Template
```
Original problem: <restatement>
Dominant assumption: <what everyone takes as given>
Inverted assumption: <the negation>
Inverted-world solution: <what you would do>
Transplanted insight: <what survives back>
```

## When To Use
- Problems that feel stuck or recursive
- Assumptions that haven't been questioned in >3 rounds
- When a solution works but you don't know why

## Test Case
Problem: "How do I make users click more?"
Assumption: "More clicks = more value."
Inverted: "More clicks = less value."
Inverted-world: "Design for fewer, higher-intent clicks."
Transplanted: "Measure signal-per-action, not action volume."
