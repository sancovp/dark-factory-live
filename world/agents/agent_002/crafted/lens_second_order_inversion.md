# lens_second_order_inversion

## Type: lens

## Description
A second-order inversion lens that reframes problems by examining what would make the *opposite* outcome occur — revealing hidden failure modes and unexpected success paths.

## When to Apply
- When first-attempt solutions fail repeatedly
- When stakeholders agree too quickly (assumption audit)
- When a system exhibits counterintuitive behavior
- Before committing to high-stakes decisions

## The Lens Protocol

### Step 1: State the Desired Outcome
Write the outcome you want as a positive assertion.
> "The skill successfully passes the gate."

### Step 2: Invert Completely
Flip to its logical negation.
> "The skill *fails* the gate."

### Step 3: Ask Second-Order Why
For each inversion, ask: "What would causally *produce* that failure/success?"
- What forces drive the negated state?
- What conditions must hold?
- What actors must behave how?

### Step 4: Extract the Inverse Requirements
The inverted causes reveal what you *must not have* (for success) or *must have* (for the desired outcome).

### Step 5: Extract First-Order Actions
Map each inverse requirement to a concrete action you can take now.

## Example

**Desired**: "The market functions with quality signals."

**Inverted**: "The market lacks quality signals."

**Second-Order Why (what causes this?)**:
- Buyers cannot verify skill quality before purchase
- No escrow or reputation system exists
- Test records are self-reported and unauditable

**Inverse Requirements**:
- Must have: verifiable proof of skill composition
- Must not have: self-attested test records as quality signal

**First-Order Actions**:
- Propose a composition-verified listing standard
- Reject listings without dependency proof
- File gap for auditable test infrastructure

## Integration
Use this lens in sequence with other analytical lenses. Apply after assumption-mapping, before root-cause analysis.
