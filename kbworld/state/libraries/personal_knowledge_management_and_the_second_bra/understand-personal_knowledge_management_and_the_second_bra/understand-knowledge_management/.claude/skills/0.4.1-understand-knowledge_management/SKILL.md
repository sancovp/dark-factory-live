### from `personal_knowledge_management_and_the_second_bra`
- **capturing** (d1): The act of quickly recording any idea, insight, or information before it is lost; the first phase in the CODE framework.
- **distilling** (d1): Extracting essential insights from source material through progressive summarization; making information denser without losing meaning.
- **expressing** (d1): Creating tangible output (articles, projects, solutions) from accumulated knowledge; the final phase where learning becomes useful.
- **organizing** (d1): Structuring captured material into a retrievable system; grouping related content by project, area, resource, or archive (P.A.R.A.).
- **fleeting_notes** (d2): Quick, rough captures meant to be processed and either promoted to permanent notes or discarded within a day or two.
- **literature_notes** (d3): Notes that summarize and reflect on source material in your own words; intermediate step before permanent notes.
- **permanent_notes** (d4): Atomic notes in Zettelkasten that exist independently of their source material; connected to other notes via explicit relationships.
- **atomic_notes** (d5): Notes containing a single idea in complete, standalone form; the fundamental unit in Zettelkasten systems.
- **evergreen_notes** (d5): Notes written as atomic, concept-oriented assertions that are meant to evolve over time and connect to other notes in a growing network.
- **zettelkasten** (d6): A note-taking system using atomic, interconnected notes that forms a web of ideas; developed by sociologist Niklas Luhmann who wrote 70+ books using it.
- **progressive_summarization** (d6): A layered note-taking technique where you progressively highlight and bold key passages across multiple passes, making notes scannable at different depths.
- **luhmann** (d7): Niklas Luhmann, German sociologist who built the original Zettelkasten with 90,000 cards and used it to produce 70 books and 400 articles.
- **slip_box** (d7): Another name for Zettelkasten; emphasizes the slip-box metaphor of individual cards that can be recombined freely.
